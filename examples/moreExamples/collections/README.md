# Examples of a book

Programs gathered from one source each: the examples of a book, and the
examples of a talk. Each folder has its own guide, with programs to open and
steps to try.

## Start here

- [Kowalski's book](kowalski-book/) — Robert Kowalski, *Computational Logic and Human Thinking* (2011): twenty-two short programs, one per example that Logical English can state.

## Try this

1. Open [the fox and the crow](kowalski-book/fox_and_crow.le?scenario=story&query=possession), from the book, and click **Query**. The fox ends up with the cheese.
2. Click the answer "fox has cheese" to see why.

# Logical Thinking in the Age of AI: example drafts

Four programs for slides 5 to 8 of the talk *Logical Thinking in the Age of
AI: A Core Competency for Society*. Two are medical: they follow a heart
failure treatment guideline. Two are ethical: they write out an argument about
provocation and test standards of judgment against it. They are drafts that
nobody has reviewed yet, and the medical ones are illustrations, not
clinical advice.

- [The talk](logical-thinking-talk/) — *Logical Thinking in the Age of AI*: a medical guideline and an argument about provocation, as rules.

## Start here

- [Which treatments does the guideline recommend?](logical-thinking-talk/heart_failure.le?scenario=paper_patient&query=treatments) — slides 5 and 6.
- [What if we know only a little about the patient?](logical-thinking-talk/heart_failure_what_if.le?scenario=what_we_know&query=combination) — a conditional answer.
- [the argument about provocation](logical-thinking-talk/leniency0.le?scenario=standard&query=one), from the talk, and click **Query**. This "standard" set of beliefs is indefensible. This is the power of logic: it lets us see the shape of an argument, independent of its packaging. There is no need to assume anything as the truth. — slide 7.
- [Is there a standard for judgment?](logical-thinking-talk/leniency.le?scenario=standard&query=one) — slide 8.
