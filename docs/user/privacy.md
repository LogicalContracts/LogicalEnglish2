# Privacy notice

*Kind: policy · Audience: everyone who uses the hosted sites · Status: current (2026-10-01)*

This notice explains what personal data the two hosted sites keep, why, and
how to have it corrected or removed. Personal data means any information
about a person who can be identified, such as an email address.

The two sites are:

- `le2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logical English editor, and
- `lps2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logic Production Systems (LPS) editor.

## Who is responsible

The sites are run by **Renting Point - Serviços de Informática, Lda.**, a
company registered in Portugal, which operates the `logicalcontracts.com`
domain. In the language of the European Union's General Data Protection
Regulation (GDPR), the company is the *controller*: the party that decides
what personal data is kept and why.

For any question or request about your data, write to
**support@logicalcontracts.com**.

## The short version

- You can use both sites without an account, and without telling us who you
  are.
- If you sign in, we learn your email address, and nothing else about you.
- We keep your email address only if you hold a licence, or if we created a
  password account for you.
- The programs you write are not kept with your name or your email address.
- No advertising, no tracking cookies, no selling or sharing of data.

## Signing in

You can sign in with a Google account, a GitHub account, or an email address
and password that we created for you.

When you sign in with Google or GitHub, you are sent to Google or GitHub, you
sign in there, and Google or GitHub tells us one thing: your email address,
after confirming that the address is really yours. We do not ask for your
name, your photograph, your contacts or anything else.

After you sign in, your browser keeps a small file called a cookie, named
`lc_session`. The cookie holds your email address and the way you signed in.
The cookie lets the two sites recognise you, and it expires after 14 days or
when you sign out. While a sign-in with Google or GitHub is in progress, a
second cookie, `lc_oauth`, lives for ten minutes at most; it makes sure that
the answer from Google or GitHub comes back to the browser that asked for it.

The sites themselves keep nothing about a person who signs in with Google or
GitHub and holds no licence. The servers note that a sign-in happened, and
by which method, but not who signed in.

## What we keep, and why

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| For a licence holder: the email address, the licence, its expiry date, and a reference to the invoice or agreement | To give the licence holder what the licence pays for | Until some months after the licence ends, or until you ask us to remove it |
| For a password account: the email address and a scrambled form of the password (a *hash*, from which the password cannot be read back) | To let that person sign in | Until the account is closed, or until you ask us to remove it |

These two lists are the only places where we keep email addresses. The legal
ground for keeping them is the licence agreement, or your own request for an
account (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).

## Your programs

To answer a query, the server receives the program you are working on, works
out the answer, and sends the answer back. The program is held in the
server's memory only while you are using it, and is not stored with your
account or your email address. Some features, such as the assistants and the
importers of other systems, write a working copy of the program to the
server's temporary space; that space is cleared when the server restarts.

## The assistants

Some features of the editors use a large language model, a program from
another company that writes text, to help you write Logical English. When you
use one of those features, the text you give the assistant, and your program
where the feature needs it, is sent to the company whose model you chose
(for example OpenAI, Anthropic or Google). That company handles the text
under its own terms. Do not give the assistants personal data you would not
want that company to receive.

## Error reports and visit counts

When something goes wrong on a site, the site sends a report of the error to
**Sentry**, a service that collects error reports. The report holds the name
of the failed operation and the error message, but not your program, your
query or your email address. The *Feedback* form also sends your message to
Sentry, with a name and an email address only if you type them in.

The sites count visits with **Cloudflare Web Analytics**. Cloudflare Web
Analytics records which pages are visited, from which country and with which
kind of browser and device. Cloudflare Web Analytics sets no cookie and does
not follow you from one site to another.

## Cookies

The sites use only the cookies needed for what you asked for:

- `lc_session` and `lc_oauth`, for signing in, described above;
- on the Logical English site, `le_ui_lang`, which remembers the language
  you chose for the menus.

None of these cookies is used for advertising or for tracking, so the sites
do not ask for your consent before setting them.

## Other companies that handle data for us

- **Fly.io** runs the two servers, in London (United Kingdom). The European
  Union recognises the United Kingdom as protecting personal data
  adequately.
- **Google** and **GitHub**, only when you choose to sign in with them.
- **Sentry** and **Cloudflare**, for error reports and visit counts.
- The company of the language model you choose, when you use an assistant.

Some of these companies may handle data outside the European Union,
including in the United States. Each one does so under its standard data
protection terms, which include the safeguards the GDPR requires for such
transfers.

## Your rights

You can ask us at any time, at **support@logicalcontracts.com**:

- for a copy of the data we keep about you;
- to correct it;
- to remove it — for a licence holder, removing the email address ends the
  licence;
- to stop using it.

We answer within one month. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you
can complain to the Portuguese data protection authority, the *Comissão
Nacional de Proteção de Dados* (CNPD), at [www.cnpd.pt](https://www.cnpd.pt),
or to the authority of the country where you live.

## Changes to this notice

When this notice changes, the date at the top changes too. A change that
affects what we keep about licence holders is also sent to them by email.
